## Scene 1 — 00:00:00–00:00:38

**Narration**

<!-- claim:01-c6 -->
When a new AI release is announced, the headline rarely answers the most practical question: can you actually use it? Access might mean a paid hosted service, a model you download and run yourself, or a demo that lets you experiment but not build a production application. One new creative platform adds another set of conditions: location, age and subscription tier. These are different routes, not interchangeable meanings of “launched.” Let’s look at what each route offers—and what the announcements do not establish.

**Visual direction**

Opening title card: “What does available mean?” Show four illustrative route labels—Hosted, Downloadable, Demo, Restricted experiment. Mark the diagram illustrative, not a product interface.

## Scene 2 — 00:00:38–00:01:17

**Narration**

<!-- claim:01-c1 -->
Google says Gemini 4 Argon is globally available to developers and enterprises through APIs, and through selected paid consumer channels. The company also states that its API supports a unified input-and-output limit of one million tokens. Tokens are the small units of text a model processes or generates; a large limit can accommodate substantial material in one exchange. But access still depends on the channel and account. Google’s announcement documents what the company says is available, not independent confirmation of the model’s performance or every user’s experience.

**Visual direction**

A labeled access card: “Google’s stated Argon access” with developer and enterprise API, and selected paid consumer channels. Add “1 million-token unified API limit — company announcement.” Do not imply free or universal consumer access.

## Scene 3 — 00:01:17–00:01:57

**Narration**

<!-- claim:01-c2 -->
Liquid AI describes a different option for its LFM2.5-24B-A2B model and base version: both are open-weight and available on Hugging Face. Open weights are downloadable learned parameters, which can let users run or adapt a model themselves. Liquid AI says the model has 24 billion parameters in total, with two billion active for each token it processes. That description does not tell us how easy it is to run on a particular computer, and open weights alone do not mean every part of a system is fully open-source.

**Visual direction**

Illustrative split card: “Hosted service” versus “Downloadable weights.” Label the model figures “Liquid AI claims: 24B total; 2B active per token.” Avoid depicting a download or running model as completed.

TII’s Falcon-ASR offers a third route. Its Hugging Face demo lets people try the speech-transcription model, while TII says API access and native applications are planned. Together, these examples show why a release label is not enough. Hosted access may depend on paid channels; downloadable weights offer control but require users to run the model; a demo is not the same as an available API; and an experimental product can have geographic, age and subscription restrictions. The evidence is mostly company descriptions, plus one early-access account. It cannot rank these systems or establish comprehensive availability, and vendor capability claims remain unverified here. Before choosing what to try, ask the practical question: which access route and deployment option can you use now?
