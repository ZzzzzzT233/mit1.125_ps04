# AI Frontier Watch: Novelty-First Editorial Policy

Revised on October 14, 2026 following the student's clarification that novelty matters more than popularity. The initial policy and its real selection results remain preserved in runs/20261014T184854Z-selection-dfc589/. This clarification does not replace the required student sample review or final content approval.

## Audience and Purpose

Serve readers interested in technology and AI; no machine learning or programming background is required.
Track new model releases, technical and research advances, and changes in companies, products, and the open-source ecosystem over the past 30 days. Explain who released what and when, what is new, what the evidence supports, and which developments suggest broader directions.
Cover language and reasoning models, multimodal generation, agents, AI coding, and related research. Build each edition around a few substantial developments and a coherent argument. Everyday applications may provide context; not every selected item must directly serve learning, work, or creative tasks.
Include smaller teams and alternative technical approaches, such as models specialized for structured decisions. Do not limit discovery to major vendors' chatbot upgrades. Explain vendor categories such as "System One" using their stated definitions rather than treating them as scientific consensus.
Write all authored assignment materials, configuration text, prompts, editorial records, articles, and video scripts in English. Use clear, accessible, measured language and explain technical terms on first use.

## Discovery and Relevance

Prioritize recent, substantive new developments and evidence-supported contributions. Identify what changed: a model release, technical method, capability, research result, access condition, product feature, or industry event. Meaningful novelty does not require a world-first invention or a frontier benchmark record. Industry and product changes may qualify without a new underlying model architecture.
Recent publication alone is insufficient: distinguish a genuinely new development or substantive new analysis from a fresh article repeating an older announcement. Record the underlying event date when available; defer claims of novelty that cannot be established from the retained evidence.
Popularity is optional discovery context, not a selection requirement, ranking criterion, or evidence of quality. Include relevant developments from smaller or less-discussed teams when their contribution is new and sufficiently supported. Do not reject an item for low views, limited coverage, or an unfamiliar producer.
If attention signals are recorded, preserve their observation dates and do not invent internet-wide popularity rankings. Reject or defer widely discussed items when they add no substantive new information or lack verifiable support.
Distinguish paper publication, demonstrations, previews, waitlists, restricted access, and general availability. Record access conditions and open-source or open-weight status. Do not describe an announcement as a launched service or equate open weights with a fully open-source release. Comparisons must identify benchmark versions, tasks, and test conditions; vendor rankings alone do not establish that a model is "the best."
Findings reflect the configured sources, not comprehensive coverage of the entire AI field.

## Freshness and Independence

Use the run metadata's UTC as-of date. Eligible substantive material must have been published from that date minus 29 days through that date, inclusive.
The date-filtering stage excludes unknown, invalid, future, and out-of-window dates. Record publication and retrieval dates separately. Identify reuploads, refreshed dates, and older events discussed in recent reporting.
Merge or explicitly reconcile a podcast episode and its YouTube copy. An RSS discovery link is not additional evidence. Reposts and repeated coverage of the same announcement do not constitute independent corroboration.

## Credibility and Uncertainty

Assess the evidence supporting each claim, rather than relying on institutional reputation, popularity, or model confidence.
Official announcements establish what a vendor announced. Label vendor performance statements as source claims and examine test conditions, limitations, and independent support.
Separate reported facts, source claims, and analytical inferences. Disclose commercial, sponsorship, and investment relationships. Seek research and practitioner perspectives and explain missing perspectives.
Where evidence conflicts, examine differences in tasks, test conditions, and use cases. Preserve unresolved uncertainty; do not force conclusions or manufacture disagreement.
Reject unsourced rumors, headline-only material, and marketing without substantive information. Defer items requiring further verification.

## Decisions, Synthesis, and References

For every eligible item, record accept, reject, or defer, a specific reason, and intended pieces. Rejected and deferred items must have an empty piece_ids array.
The three pieces address which models and products matter, what technical advances and supporting evidence they offer, and which directions emerge across developments. Each piece needs at least three independent sources, one insight connecting sources, and a meaningful limitation. Trend claims require evidence across sources; distinguish observed changes from predictions.
Connect every substantive claim to an item_id, version_id, and exact quotation or transcript segment_id. Preserve timestamps for audio and video references.
Generate articles and video scripts from the same briefs and reviewed evidence. Do not present speculation as established fact.
Review at least three accepted and three rejected or deferred items, preserve correction reasons, revise this policy, and rerun affected stages. Only the student supplies final approval of the actual content.
