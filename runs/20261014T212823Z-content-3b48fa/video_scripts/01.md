## Scene 3 — 00:01:17–00:02:08

**Narration**

<!-- claim:01-c3 -->
Liquid AI offers a different route. The company says its LFM2.5-24B-A2B reasoning model is open-weight and downloadable now. Open-weight means the model’s learned numerical settings can be obtained; it does not, by itself, mean every part of a project is open source. Liquid AI describes 24 billion total parameters, with two billion active per token. A token is a small unit of text, such as a word fragment. Parameters are internal values a model uses to process information; here, the active count indicates that only part of the model is used for each token. Downloading may suit people who want to run a model locally, but that depends on their hardware and setup.

**Visual direction**

Illustrative diagram: a full network with a smaller selected portion highlighted per token. Label figures as Liquid AI claims and label diagram illustrative.

## Scene 4 — 00:02:08–00:02:51

**Narration**

<!-- claim:01-c4 -->
<!-- claim:01-c5 -->
TII’s Falcon-ASR is a speech-recognition model with a more limited access offer: TII says people can try it in a Hugging Face demo, while API access and native applications are planned. Planned is not the same as available now. A separate Hugging Face article describes ML Intern, an agent-assisted workflow for building task-specific models. Its author reports that the workflow helped train, evaluate, and publish models, including a 0.8-billion-parameter prompt rewriter that runs on a CPU—the computer’s general-purpose processor. That is an author-reported project, not independent confirmation that other users will get the same result.

Together, the releases offer distinct choices: paid hosted access, downloadable weights, a demo with fuller access still pending, and a workflow for making a specialized model. They do not tell us which option users prefer, or which model performs best. The product descriptions and project results largely come from the companies or authors involved, and access conditions can change. So before trying one, ask: what can I use today, what does it require, and what evidence supports the result I care about?
